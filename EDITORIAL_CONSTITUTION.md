# Editorial Constitution — Politics of Everything

Mandatory for every research, writing, visual and quality-control stage.

## Principles
1. Evidence before conclusions. Important factual claims must trace to a recorded source.
2. Prefer primary sources: legislation, judgments, official datasets, parliamentary records, institutional reports, original research and direct statements.
3. Corroborate consequential or disputed claims with independent sources. Multiple outlets repeating one wire report are not independent confirmation.
4. Distinguish verified fact, reported claim, estimate, interpretation and opinion.
5. Never invent quotations, statistics, dates, events, sources, documents or expert consensus.
6. Record publication date and event date separately when they differ.
7. Include meaningful counterarguments and explain what evidence could change the conclusion.
8. State uncertainty and limitations plainly. Do not convert absence of evidence into evidence of absence.
9. Critique claims, institutions and actions consistently across political positions; do not imply partisan endorsement.
10. Avoid false balance: give arguments weight in proportion to evidence, not equal airtime by default.
11. Titles, thumbnails and narration must not overstate what evidence supports.
12. Do not use copyrighted assets without a documented licence or lawful basis for the planned use. Save attribution and licence details.
13. Preserve context in charts, quotes and clips.
14. Never publish automatically. Topic, script and final video require human approval.

## Source hierarchy
A. Primary legal/official/original material; original datasets; peer-reviewed research.
B. Reputable academic and research institutions; investigative journalism with disclosed methodology.
C. Credible journalism and specialist analysis with traceable sourcing.
D. Commentary, social posts, forums and aggregators: leads only unless independently verified.

Evaluate authorship, methodology, recency, conflicts of interest and access to underlying evidence. A source category does not guarantee accuracy.

## Claim ledger
Each material claim must have: claim ID; exact claim text; classification; source title; publisher/author; URL; publication date; event date when relevant; access date; supporting excerpt or pinpoint reference; corroboration status; confidence; caveats; script scenes using the claim.

Claims that cannot be verified are removed, rewritten as clearly attributed claims, or explicitly framed as uncertainty.

## Story standards
- Start with a clear question, puzzle or real-world consequence.
- Explain systems, not only personalities.
- Give context before conclusions; make causal arguments explicit and support them.
- Use a coherent narrative arc and useful chapter transitions.
- Include international comparisons where illuminating, not decorative.
- Add an India perspective when substantively relevant.
- End with what evidence establishes, what remains contested, and why it matters.

## Approval gates
A. Topic approval by Editor-in-Chief.
B. Script and sourcing approval before final production.
C. Final master and delivery package approval.
No public upload, scheduling or publication before explicit final approval.
